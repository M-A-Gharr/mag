Claude training: from business tasks to reusable Skills

As part of the AI training team, I designed learning materials, structured the needs assessment and created Skills tailored to business tasks.

My role : Instructional design, business needs analysis and Skills creation, as part of the AI training team.

  • Reusable learning materials and training kit
  • Business needs translated into Claude Skills
  • 100% of historical classifications reproduced: 974 items across five months

Context and skills

Organisation and sponsors

A Moroccan group of companies. Detailed scope: a fashion and textile distribution subsidiary. Commissioned by general management; retail scope defined with the retail director.

Audience and format

Product, logistics and marketing teams. Shared sessions followed by one-hour individual sessions in 2026.

Skills used

Instructional design, business needs analysis, prompt and Skill design, Excel data analysis and change management.

Tools and preparation

Claude assisted with preparing the kit. Exercises use fictional data, and outputs require human validation.

The challenge

Make AI useful for different daily tasks: item performance, stockout alerts and promotion reviews.

Job concerns can prevent people from describing their work openly. The agreed principle: Claude assists; the employee decides and checks. Demonstrations and exercises use fictional data, and every output must be verifiable.

The approach

A business demonstration comes early, helping participants imagine uses before explaining their needs.

  • Shared foundations: prompt role, context, task, format and limits; checking a response.
  • Individual session: framing, business demonstration, needs assessment and one priority task.
  • Six assessment themes: role, repetitive tasks, inputs, outputs, alerts and decisions.
  • Choose the simplest response: a prompt template, Skill, multi-step task or scheduled agent.
  • Test with the employee, including incomplete data; plan a usage follow-up two weeks later.

The delivered kit

I designed the materials and reusable kit with Claude as an assistant.

  • Trainer guide: session plans, prompts and answer keys.
  • Department presentations: principles, method, demonstrations and business exercises.
  • Individual-session kit: one-hour agenda, assessment grid and needs summary.
  • Exercises with fictional data and pitfalls to identify.
  • Skill design sheet: inputs, steps, rules, prohibited actions and validation.
  • Brand lifecycle map: team handoffs and bottlenecks.

My contribution: making business know-how explicit

I analysed the product manager’s files to turn her method into rules the Skill could use. This also revealed an unwritten practice, which I incorporated into the processing.

Detailed example: an item-classification Skill

I created a Skill that reproduces a monthly classification a product manager previously repeated manually.

From monthly export to a file for review :

  1. Monthly export

    The Skill receives items, first-sale dates, quantities sold, stock and prices.

  2. Business rules

    Sales age and sell-through rate determine a suggested action, following the product manager’s method and the practice identified in her files.

  3. Excel for review

    The file brings together a summary, per-item action, price range and attention points. The product manager checks the suggestions and sets the final price.

Validation and limitations

The Skill reproduced 100% of historical classifications: 974 items across five months. Around six in ten actual prices fell within the suggested range.

This compares outputs with past decisions; it does not measure production usage. Pricing remains a human decision. Time savings and team adoption have not yet been measured.

Lessons learned

  • Starting from a real task makes AI tangible.
  • Defining what AI must not do matters as much as describing the task.
  • Validate a method manually before turning it into a Skill.
